College of IT runs training course for instructors

The Zayed University Cisco Regional Academy, managed by the College of Information Technology, has conducted a training course in Cisco's IT Essentials curriculum, which covers the fundamentals of computer hardware and software as well as advanced concepts.

The course was offered for instructors from Al Mawakeb Schools (Barsha and Garhoud campuses), The International School for Arts & Sciences (ISAS), Manipal University, Preston University, as well as from ZU. In their role as Cisco Local Academies supported by the ZU Regional Academy, these schools and universities will in turn be delivering this course to their students. Students who complete the course will be able to describe the internal components of a computer, assemble a computer system, install an operating system, and troubleshoot using system tools and diagnostic software. Students will also be able to connect to the internet and share resources in a network environment.

The Networking Academy Program is an internationally recognized program that was initiated by Cisco, a leading supplier of networking devices and network management tools. The program is based on an e-learning model that includes Web-based educational, media-rich content, online testing, student performance tracking and instructor training and support, as well as hands-on laboratories. It is operated through, and in conjunction with, technology learning centers such as schools, colleges, universities and occupational training facilities, on a 'not for profit' basis.

Zayed University has been part of this program since 2004. It operates as both a Regional and Local Networking Academy. As a Regional Academy, Zayed University supports private and public high schools and universities who want to take part in the program as Local Academies to deliver the Networking Academy curricula. Training, technical and other program support is provided to affiliated schools to ensure high quality of program delivery. As a Local Academy, Zayed University also offers the networking curriculum to its own students, to prepare them to receive the industry-recognized Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) qualification.

ZU Hosts KHDA Year 12 Summer School

Zayed University’s Academic Bridge Program and the British Council ran an English summer school for high school students during the summer. The eight-week program, which was established and funded by the Dubai Schools Agency of the Knowledge and Human Development Authority, aimed to improve students’ English language and academic skills, as well as reduce the amount of time that they would need to spend in foundation English programs. Any Grade 12 student from a government school who had a minimum CEPA score of 160 was eligible to join the program free of charge.

The British Council took the male students, and 106 female students attended the program held by ZU’s Academic Bridge Program; approximately three quarters of these students have registered to study at Zayed University.

The program run by the Academic Bridge Program (ABP) was remarkable. Not only did the students feel that they had made a lot of progress, but all of the students who attended regularly have entered the ABP at least one level higher. Also, 47 of the students were able to take the IELTS examination at the end of the course and, of these students, 22 achieved a high enough standard to be eligible to bypass the ABP altogether. For more information about the KHDA Year 12 Summer School, see http://www.ameinfo.com/164139.html

ZU Assistant Professor Displays Art Installation in Beijing

Janet Bellotto, Assistant Professor in Zayed University’s College of Arts and Sciences, has presented her work, “A Tale Remains”, at the Chinese Character Biennial in Beijing, China.

Curated by Kóan Jeff Baysa, New York, and Xing Lei Pan, Beijing, the exhibition is hosted by KU Art Center. Coinciding with the Art Olympics, the exhibition, which opened on August 2 and runs until September 6, 2008, includes 40 artists from China, Vietnam, Germany, Canada, Australia, Korea, and more. Janet’s work consists of an installation of whale-like bones, which visitors walk through and write out their messages or memories on small bones. As the people who lost their lives, homes, identities, and their histories – in disasters of New Orleans and recently in China’s Sichuan province – the space within “A Tale Remains” provides a ground for remembering and recollecting the lost.

“A Tale Remains” will travel to Toronto in November. Visit www.kuartcenter.com for more information.

CIT Students Successfully Complete Summer Courses

This Summer, the College of Information Technology offered a number of courses for its students on both campuses. The courses ran over six weeks from June 22 till July 30, and attracted over 50 students. CIT offered a course on networking certification (Dubai) and a course on Systems Analysis and Design (Abu Dhabi).

The networking certification course is part of the Cisco Regional Academy Program. This course provided in-depth theory and challenging hands-on labs for students to learn about the fundamentals of data networking. By the end of the course, eight students received the CCNA1 certification.

The Systems Analysis and Design course provided an understanding of information system design and development processes. It enabled students to see the important connect between Business and Information Technology. A highlight of the course for the 20 students was a site visit to ADGAS to see first hand the role of Systems Analysis in an organization and to meet and talk with a Business IS Analyst.

The college has also offered Internships, Independent Study and Capstone Seminar courses over the Summer. Dedicated faculty and eager students have helped make the summer courses a big success. Driven by a growing student demand, it is anticipated that the College of IT will be offering additional courses next summer.

Based on student feedback, taking summer courses makes sense because it allows students to complete a course from their IT degree program in an environment they are familiar with, to catch up on courses in their schedules and in some cases graduate earlier.

Zayed University is accredited by the Middle States Commission on Higher Education

His Excellency Sheikh Nahayan Mabarak Al Nahayan, Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research and President of Zayed University has announced that Zayed University is accredited by the Middle States Commission on Higher Education.

His Excellency Sheikh Nahayan said during a press conference held at his palace in Al Khazna area: “This is a very significant and important achievement for Zayed University especially as we have just celebrated it’s 10th anniversary. This accreditation was the result of more than five years of hard and intensive work and cooperation with the Middle States Commission on Higher Education, during which the commission studied, reviewed and assessed the university’s institutional and administrative efficiency as a higher educational institution, the suitability of the curriculum in both baccalaureate and masters programs, as well as faculty and staff quality, consistent standards for student admissions, and appropriate orientation and placement systems.

Dr. Sulaiman Al Jassim, Vice President of Zayed University, stressed the importance of this accreditation to the University as it emphasizes Zayed University’s position as a leading university in the region, embodying the same rigorous standards and intellectual elements found in major universities throughout the world.

The Goodwill Cook Book receives an award

A Zayed University publication has received a "Special Award of the Jury" at the Gourmand World Cookbook Awards, held in London on 13 April 2008. The Jury praised The Goodwill Cook Book for its success in raising funds for the victims of the Indian Ocean Tsunami. The book was produced by ZU's Publications Department in the wake of the 2004 disaster. Leveraging the talents and goodwill of ZU students, faculty and staff, the book was distributed within weeks and raised more than 36,000 AED for relief efforts in Aceh, Indonesia. The Editor of the Goodwill Cook Book, Brian O'Flynn, hailed the announcement of the award as a vindication of grassroots action for good: "The award is a tribute to the combined efforts of the ZU community and the tenacity of all those affected by the Tsunami to whom I dedicate this award".
